# Look Busy — read before every change

A PS1-style 3D office game: you just joined Humble House Games, walk the
floors, sit at desks and play the real games on their monitors (Pong, the
stand-up game, a cube, a slot-machine roguelite, a theorem prover, arcade
cages, a trading floor), while Dale guides you and the boss watches. Godot
4.3, GDScript only.

Samuel builds from his phone: Claude writes and pushes the code, GitHub builds a
web version, and he playtests it in Safari. Keep replies short and in plain words,
and handle git, builds and fixes yourself.
**End every reply with the play link:** https://humble-house-studios.github.io/look-busy/

## The scaling rule
The web build in Safari is the test for every platform. If it looks and plays
right there, it's right on iPhone, Android and Steam. To keep that true:

- **Phones = vertical. Steam = horizontal.** Same game, same code, two layouts.
  - Base resolution 720×1280, stretch mode `canvas_items`, aspect `expand`.
    When the screen is wider than tall, `main.gd` swaps the base to 1280×720
    so text stays readable sideways.
  - Mobile exports lock to portrait (`display/window/handheld/orientation`).
  - Desktop opens a 1280×720 window (`window_width_override.pc` /
    `window_height_override.pc`).
  - The UI picks its layout from the screen shape (wider than tall = landscape
    layout) and switches live on resize. Build it from Control nodes, anchors
    and containers, never fixed pixel positions.
  - Testing both on a phone: hold it upright for the mobile layout, turn it
    sideways for the Steam layout.
- **Compatibility renderer everywhere**, so the web build looks exactly like the
  shipped apps. The game isn't graphics-heavy, so nothing is lost.
- **Nothing web-only.** No browser-only APIs; anything platform-specific is
  wrapped in `OS.has_feature(...)` with a working fallback.
- **Touch and mouse both work** for every action; touch targets at least 88 px
  tall at 720 wide (cards in hand included). Respect the phone's safe area so
  nothing hides under the notch.
- **Stay light:** web build under ~30 MB, no reliance on threads.

## Mini games: this repo is the source of truth
- Every mini game lives in `addons/hh_minigames/<game>/` (standup, claw,
  pusher, fall_guy, rage_cage, trading, golf, pitch, open_mic): one entry
  scene `main.tscn`, a README, its tests. Read `addons/hh_minigames/README.md`
  before touching one.
- Same interface everywhere: `start(config)` before adding the scene, a
  `finished(result)` signal (`{game, score, outcome, ...}`). The office's
  economy goes in as `config.host` (duck typed). Look games up with
  `HHMinigames` (`scene_for`, `script_for`).
- **No mini game may use office code** (no `res://` path outside the addon, no
  office class). `addons/hh_minigames/tests/test_interface.gd` fails the build
  if one does. No autoloads in the addon.
- killin-in-comedy gets a copy: `.github/workflows/sync-minigames.yml` copies
  the folder to its `sync/minigames` branch and opens/updates a PR on every
  push here that changes it. Never edit the copy over there.
- The stand-up game's design (the comedy MVP rules) is
  `addons/hh_minigames/standup/DESIGN.md`.

## Code structure
- Office logic in `res://engine/`, with no scene dependencies, tested headless
  with GUT (`tests/` and every `addons/hh_minigames/*/tests/`).
- Placeholder art only: colored panels and labels. No image generation.
- **Controllers:** `addons/hh_minigames/_shared/pad_cursor.gd` (a copy lives in Loot, Proof and Cube) puts a
  frame on the buttons: D-pad/stick moves, A presses; buttons can carry meta
  `pad_button` (X = Land It, Start = pause, B = skip). The office walks on the
  sticks, A sits, Back stands, and while seated pushes the pad into that game.
  Games read the pad from events, never `Input` polling (it would leak across desks).
- Ask before adding any addon beyond GUT (and our own `hh_minigames`).
- Sounds and music are generated by `tools/make_audio.py` (numpy + ffmpeg);
  re-run it after editing. Sounds the mini games use land in the addon
  (`_shared/audio/`, `standup/audio/`, `rage_cage/audio/`), the office's in `audio/`. Effects are WAV, the music loop is OGG.
- **The office** (`scenes/office/`, the main scene): a PS1-style 3D office (wobbly, dithered,
  hand-made boxes) lit at golden hour (low sun through the blinds, warm
  shadows; lights in `ps1.gdshader`), where you walk up and play the real
  games on the desk monitors, first person (the main desk runs the stand-up game, `addons/hh_minigames/standup/`). Every computer is a chunky beige 90s
  CRT (4:3); each game runs in a 90s desktop window on it (wallpaper, title bar,
  taskbar), built by `_crt()`, `_desktop()` and `_window_frame()` in office.gd.
  A/B test: SCREENS in the pause menu (or L) flips to look B, slim charcoal
  CRT TVs with rabbit ears (`_tv()`), the game straight on the glass; each
  look's shells live in `_style()` groups, screens move to that look's spot,
  and the choice is remembered in `user://office_look.cfg`. The Loot Machine
  stays its original tall cabinet in both looks. The game runs unchanged
  in a SubViewport; clicks/taps are ray-cast onto the screen. On touch, tapping
  the computer (or SIT DOWN) glides the camera to a seated view that frames the
  screen; STAND UP returns to walking. A side desk runs Phone Pong
  (`humble-house-studios/phone-pong`), pulled into `res://games/pong/` by
  `tools/fetch_games.sh` at build time (not committed; run it before testing the
  office locally). Pong runs in an embedded Window so its window logic works
  untouched; its window on the CRT goes wide when the phone turns, so Pong plays
  its landscape mode (paddle under the right thumb), and while seated it takes
  touches anywhere. A third desk (right wall, under a chalkboard) runs Proof
  Hand (`humble-house-studios/proof-hand`, full runs: antes, bosses, the
  Library, Axioms) in `res://games/proof/` the same way (the Loot link's
  `tick_proof` gives its runs relics and pays tokens); it needs its `Game`/`Save` autoloads, which `fetch_games.sh
  --autoloads` adds to project.godot (CI only; locally `git checkout
  project.godot` after). Other games' autoload names and class_names must not
  clash with ours. Each game's sound fades with distance. The **Loot Machine** cabinet by the right wall

  same way): a slot-machine roguelite whose office relics power up every game.
  `OfficeLink` (`games/loot/ecosystem/office_link.gd`) is ticked by the office

  Machine run → prove a theorem → promotion: the door opens), shown as a
